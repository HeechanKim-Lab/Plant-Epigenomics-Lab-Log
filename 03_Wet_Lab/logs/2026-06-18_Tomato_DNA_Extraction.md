# Tomato DNA Extraction Log (2026-06-18)

## Reference Protocol

### 1. Genomic DNA Extraction (CTAB Method)

* **1)** Prepare sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes (e-tubes) and collect the tomato leaf samples into each designated tube. 
* **2)** Add 1 mL of CTAB Buffer and 5 µL of $\beta$-Mercaptoethanol to each tube, then thoroughly grind the leaf tissue sample.
* **3)** Incubate the mixtures at 65°C for 1 hour using a digital dry bath or water bath. 
    * *Note:* To ensure uniform lysis, invert the tubes 10 times every 10 minutes throughout the incubation period.
* **4)** Vortex the incubated samples thoroughly.
* **5)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. 
    * *Note:* Evaluate the pellet and phase separation state after the run; an additional 3 minutes of centrifugation may be applied if the sample state requires further clarification.
* **6)** Carefully transfer 700 µL of the cleared supernatant to a new 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tube.
* **7)** Add 700 µL of PCI (Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol = 25:24:1) to the recovered supernatant to achieve a 1:1 volumetric ratio.
* **8)** Mix the solution completely by inverting the tube 10 times, then centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C.
* **9)** Recover 500 µL of the upper aqueous supernatant and transfer it into a fresh 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tube.
* **10)** Add an equal volume (500 µL) of PCI directly to the transferred supernatant.
* **11)** Mix by inverting the tube 10 times, then centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C.
* **12)** Transfer exactly 450 µL of the final clear aqueous supernatant into a new 1.5 mL e-tube.
* **13)** Add 450 µL of Isopropanol (Isopropyl Alcohol) to maintain a precise 1:1 ratio, and mix thoroughly by inverting.
* **14)** Incubate the final precipitation mixture at -20°C overnight to maximize DNA yield.

## Pre-Run Preparations

* **Consumables (Per Sample):** 2 × 1.5 mL tubes / 2 × 2.0 mL tubes
* **Equipment:** Digital Dry Bath (Pre-heated to 65°C) / Motorized hand-drill
* **Disinfection:** 70% EtOH wipe tissues (for drill tip cleaning between samples)

  * 36 × 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tubes (allocated for Step 6 first PCI extraction and Step 9 second PCI extraction).

### 2. Sample Preparation, Reagent Loading, and Handheld Drill Homogenization

* **Sample Pre-Treatment:** Gathered 18 distinct tomato leaf samples. Carefully inspected each leaf and removed any surface debris, soil particles, or external contaminants to ensure downstream DNA purity.
* **Initial Lysis Charge (Protocol Deviation for Splashing Control):** * Rather than adding the full 1 mL of CTAB buffer immediately, added only **300 µL of CTAB Buffer** and **5 µL of $\beta$-Mercaptoethanol (BME)** to each 1.5 mL tube containing the leaf tissue.
  * *Rationale:* Restricting the initial liquid volume to 300 µL effectively prevents sample overflow, foaming, and centrifugal splashing  during the high-speed mechanical drilling process.
* **Mechanical Homogenization:** Pulverized the leaf tissue directly inside the e-tube using a motorized handheld drill until the sample was completely disrupted and homogeneous.
* **Secondary Buffer Replenishment:** Immediately after drilling, added the residual **700 µL of CTAB Buffer** to each tube, bringing the final extraction volume to the standard 1 mL required for complete cellular lysis.
* **Cross-Contamination Control:** To guarantee sample integrity across the 18 independent replicates, the drill tip was thoroughly sanitized with an ethanol (EtOH) wipe between every single sample processing step to eliminate any cross-sample tissue or nucleic acid carryover.

### 3. Dry Bath Incubation and Mechanical Agitation

* **Initial Homogenization Check:** Inverted all 18 tubes manually right after the final CTAB buffer addition to ensure the freshly added buffer and the drilled leaf tissue slurry were completely mixed.
* **Thermal Lysis:** Transferred the tubes to a digital dry bath pre-heated and stabilized at **65°C**, incubating the samples for exactly **1 hour** to achieve complete cellular and nuclear membrane disruption.
* **Intermittent Agitation Cycle:** Maintained strict kinetic control during the thermal lysis phase by removing the tubes and manually **inverting them for 10 seconds every 10 minutes** throughout the 1-hour incubation. 
  * *Rationale:* Continuous, scheduled inversion prevents sample sedimentation, optimizes the chemical action of the surfactant, and ensures uniform heat distribution across all replicates.
  

### 4. Post-Incubation Vortexing and Primary Centrifugation

* **Homogenization:** Thoroughly vortexed all 18 samples immediately following the dry bath incubation to ensure complete uniformity of the lysate before clearing cellular debris.
* **Primary Centrifugation Run:** Loaded the tubes into the centrifuge and spun them at **13,000 RPM for 10 minutes at 4°C**.
* **Quality Control Check & Technical Spin Extension:** * Following the initial 10-minute run, a visual inspection revealed that the phase boundary and pellet compaction were still ambiguous and poorly defined.
  * *Action:* Executed an **additional 3-minute centrifugation step** under identical conditions (13,000 RPM, 4°C) to ensure full sedimentation of the insoluble plant materials and a distinct boundary layer.


### 5. Primary Supernatant Recovery

* **Phase Recovery:** Carefully aspirated the cleared aqueous supernatant from the 1.5 mL e-tubes following the extended centrifugation step.
* **Volumetric Transfer:** Transferred exactly **700 µL** of the supernatant into the first designated set of fresh, pre-labeled 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tubes, taking care to avoid disturbing the underlying pellet of plant cell debris.


### 6. First Organic Phase Separation (PCI Extraction)

* **Reagent Addition:** Added exactly 700 µL of PCI (Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol = 25:24:1) to each 2.0 mL tube containing the recovered supernatant to maintain a strict 1:1 volumetric ratio.
* **Emulsification:** Inverted the tubes manually for 10 seconds to thoroughly mix the organic and aqueous phases, promoting efficient protein denaturation.
* **Centrifugation & Protocol Deviation:** Centrifuged all 18 samples at **12,000 RPM for 10 minutes at 4°C**.
  * *Protocol Deviation:* The centrifugation speed was altered to 12,000 RPM, deviating from the 13,000 RPM specified in Step 8 of the reference protocol.


### 7. Secondary Aqueous Phase Recovery and Second PCI Extraction

* **Aqueous Phase Transfer:** Recovered **500 µL** of the upper clear aqueous supernatant from the post-centrifugation mixture and transferred it into the second designated set of fresh, pre-labeled 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tubes.
  * *Note on Volumetric Alignment:* Aligned the supernatant volume to 500 µL to maintain the strict 1:1 ratio required by the subsequent reagent addition.
* **Secondary Organic Extraction:** Added an equal volume of **500 µL of PCI** (Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol = 25:24:1) directly to the transferred supernatant to perform a secondary protein cleanup.


### 8. Secondary Organic Phase Separation and Centrifugation

* **Emulsification:** Inverted the 2.0 mL tubes manually for 10 seconds to thoroughly emulsify the second PCI volume with the aqueous supernatant, ensuring complete protein denaturation and boundary preparation.
* **Centrifugation & Protocol Deviation:** Centrifuged all 18 samples at **13,000 RPM for 10 minutes at 4°C**.
  * *Protocol Deviation:* The centrifugation speed was increased to 13,000 RPM, deviating from the 12,000 RPM specified in Step 11 of the reference protocol.


### 9. Final Supernatant Recovery and Alcohol Precipitation

* **Final Aqueous Phase Transfer:** Extracted exactly **450 µL** of the final highly clear aqueous supernatant from the 2.0 mL tubes and transferred it into the second designated set of fresh, pre-labeled 1.5 mL e-tubes.
* **Precipitation Reagent Addition:** Added an equal volume of **450 µL of Isopropanol** (Isopropyl Alcohol) directly to the transferred supernatant to establish the mandatory 1:1 volumetric ratio.
* **Precipitation Assembly:** Inverted the tubes manually several times to ensure a completely homogeneous mixture, initiating the structural precipitation of the genomic DNA from the solution.

### 10. Overnight DNA Precipitation

* **Incubation Conditions:** Transferred all 18 fully assembled precipitation tubes directly into a **-20°C freezer** for an **overnight incubation**.
* **Rationale:** Maintained structural precipitation overnight to ensure absolute aggregation and maximize the total recovery yield of the high-molecular-weight tomato genomic DNA from the solution.
