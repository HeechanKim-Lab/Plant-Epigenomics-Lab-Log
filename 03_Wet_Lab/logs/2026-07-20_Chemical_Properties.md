#### 2. Protein Extraction Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)



1. **Sample Suspension:** Transfer pulverized tissue powder into a microcentrifuge tube and add 300 µL of extraction buffer (maintain approximately 1:1 powder-to-buffer ratio).

2. **Homogenization:** Vortex thoroughly to ensure complete sample dissolution and homogenization within the buffer.

3. **Clarification Centrifugation:** Centrifuge at 13,000 RPM for 20–30 minutes at 4°C to pellet insoluble cellular debris.

4. **Supernatant Isolation:** Carefully aspirate and transfer 200 µL of clarified supernatant into a fresh microcentrifuge tube.

5. **Quantification & Storage:** Quantify protein concentration, normalize samples to uniform concentrations, aliquot into 20 µL working volumes, and store at -20°C for downstream applications.



## Chemical Profiles & Reagent Characterization

---

### Tris-HCl (Tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane Hydrochloride)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{NO}_3 \cdot \text{HCl}$ | **MW:** 157.60 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Primary pH buffering agent (20 mM final concentration, pH 7.5).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Primary amine salt with a $\text{p}K_a$ of 8.07 at 25°C.
  * Temperature-dependent $\text{p}K_a$ shift ($\Delta\text{p}K_a / \Delta T \approx -0.031 / \text{°C}$); pH increases as solution temperature drops to 4°C.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Maintains physiological pH during cellular lysis, buffering against organic acids released from vacuolar rupture and stabilizing protein tertiary structures.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Causes mild skin and eye irritation.
  * Always adjust stock pH at the intended operating temperature or compensate for temperature coefficient shifts.

---

### EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic Acid, Disodium Salt)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{14}\text{N}_2\text{Na}_2\text{O}_8 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | **MW:** 372.24 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Metalloprotease inhibitor auxiliary / Chelation agent (1 mM final concentration, pH 8.0).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Hexadentate aminopolycarboxylic acid chelating agent.
  * High binding affinity for divalent metal cations ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Depletes free divalent cations ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$) essential for metalloprotease and nucleolytic enzymatic activity, preventing enzymatic protein degradation post-lysis.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Free acid form is insoluble in water at pH < 8.0; requires addition of $\text{NaOH}$ to achieve full dissolution during stock preparation.
  * Eye and respiratory irritant.

---

### Sodium Chloride ($\text{NaCl}$)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{NaCl}$ | **MW:** 58.44 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Ionic strength regulator / Protein solubilizer (150 mM final concentration).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Inorganic ionic salt, completely dissociating into $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ in aqueous solutions.
  * High solubility in water ($\approx 360 \text{ g/L}$ at 20°C).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Provides ionic strength matching physiological levels to promote protein solubility via salting-in effects and prevent non-specific electrostatic protein aggregation.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Hygroscopic; store stock in a sealed vessel under ambient conditions.
  * Non-hazardous under standard laboratory conditions.

---

### Triton X-100

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O})_n$ ($n \approx 9\text{--}10$) | **MW:** $\approx 625 \text{ g/mol}$
* **Role in Protocol:** Non-ionic detergent (0.1% v/v final concentration).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Non-ionic surfactant containing a hydrophilic polyethylene oxide chain and a aromatic hydrocarbon hydrophobic group.
  * Critical Micelle Concentration (CMC): 0.2–0.9 mM (0.015%–0.06% v/v).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Permeabilizes lipid bilayers by solubilizing membrane lipids without disrupting native non-covalent protein interactions, facilitating the extraction of membrane-bound and cytosolic proteins.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * High viscosity requires slow pipetting or positive-displacement pipettes for accurate delivery.
  * Toxic to aquatic organisms; suspected endocrine disruptor. Dispose of via hazardous chemical waste streams.

---

### DTT (Dithiothreitol)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_2\text{S}_2$ | **MW:** 154.25 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Small-molecule reducing agent (5 mM final concentration).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Dithiol derivative (Cleland's reagent); redox potential $E^{\circ\prime} = -0.33 \text{ V}$ at pH 7.0.
  * Forms a thermodynamically stable six-membered ring with an intramolecular disulfide bond upon oxidation.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Reduces intra- and inter-molecular disulfide bonds ($-\text{S}\text{--}\text{S}-$) to free sulfhydryl groups ($-\text{SH}$), preserving protein monomeric states and preventing oxidation-induced aggregation.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Thermally unstable and rapidly oxidizes in aqueous solutions exposed to air; store stock solutions at -20°C and add fresh to buffer immediately prior to extraction.
  * Pungent odor; harmful if swallowed, causes skin and respiratory irritation.

---

### SDS (Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{25}\text{SO}_4\text{Na}$ | **MW:** 288.38 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Anionic denaturing detergent (0.1% w/v final concentration).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Organosulfur anionic surfactant with a 12-carbon hydrophobic tail and a polar sulfate headgroup.
  * CMC: $\approx 8 \text{ mM}$ (0.23% w/v) in pure water at 25°C; decreases in ionic buffers.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Disruption of non-covalent secondary and tertiary protein structures, denaturing native conformations and imparting a uniform negative charge-to-mass ratio along the polypeptide backbone.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Fine powder presents a severe inhalation hazard; weigh under a certified chemical fume hood.
  * Subject to precipitation at low temperatures (<10°C) or in solutions containing high concentrations of potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$).

---

### Liquid Nitrogen ($\text{LN}_2$)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{N}_2$ | **MW:** 28.01 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Cryogenic tissue pulverization medium.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Liquefied elemental nitrogen gas; boiling point $-195.79\text{ °C}$ (77.36 K).
  * Extremely low viscosity and high expansion ratio ($\approx 1:694$ liquid to gas at standard temperature and pressure).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Vitrifies tissue structures rapidly to arrest metabolic activity, prevent proteolysis, and render plant cell wall polymers brittle for mechanical grinding.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Severe cryogenic burn hazard; wear insulated cryogenic gloves and splash-resistant eye protection.
  * Asphyxiation risk due to rapid oxygen displacement in unventilated spaces.
  * Never store in sealed, unvented containers to prevent catastrophic pressure buildup.


## 2. Tomato RNA Extraction

### Reference Protocol

#### 1. Tomato RNA Extraction Protocol

* **1)** Grind 0.1 g of the sample using liquid nitrogen (LN2).
    * *Note:* All reagents and equipment must be treated with DEPC for at least 2 days and then autoclaved before use. (DEPC preparation: Add 1 mL of DEPC to 1 L of Distilled Water, allow it to dissolve for at least 2 days, then autoclave).
* **2)** Add the ground sample to a 2 mL tube containing 1 mL of TRIzol, vortex thoroughly, and incubate at room temperature (RT) for 5 minutes.
    * *Note 2-1 (Mechanism):* TRIzol consists of phenol, guanidinium, and thiocyanate. Phenol denatures proteins, dissolves lipids, disrupts cell walls, and denatures DNA.
* **3)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **4)** Transfer the supernatant (~1 mL) to a new DEPC-treated microcentrifuge tube (e-tube) and incubate at RT for 5 minutes.
* **5)** Add 200 µL of chloroform, mix by inverting, and incubate at RT for 2 minutes.
    * *Note 5-1 (Mechanism):* Chloroform separates the phases containing proteins and other materials dissolved in TRIzol, facilitating cleaner RNA isolation.
* **6)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **7)** Transfer the supernatant (~500 µL) to a new microcentrifuge tube and add 500 µL of Isopropanol.
    * *Note 7-1 (Mechanism):* Isopropanol exploits the low solubility of nucleic acids in alcohol to efficiently precipitate the RNA.
* **8)** Incubate for 5 minutes, then centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **9)** Discard the supernatant and add 1 mL of 75% DEPC-treated ethanol.
* **10)** Incubate at -20°C for 2 hours OR overnight.
* **11)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **12)** Discard the supernatant, air-dry the pellet completely until the ethanol evaporates (do not exceed 30 minutes), and resuspend by adding 50 µL of DEPC-treated Distilled Water (D.W.).
* **13)** Store at -80°C.

#### 2. DNase Treatment

##### Reaction Mixture

| Component | Volume |
| :--- | :---: |
| 10X Reaction Buffer (Salt) | 3 µL |
| 10X TURBO DNase | 2 µL |
| Total RNA | 25 µL |
| **Total Volume** | **30 µL** |

##### Protocol

* **1)** Mix gently by pipetting, then incubate at 37°C for 30 minutes.
* **2)** Add 270 µL of DEPC-treated Distilled Water (D.W.) to bring the total volume up to 300 µL.
* **3)** Add 300 µL of PCI (Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol = 25:24:1) (equal to the total volume) and mix by inverting.
* **4)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **5)** Transfer 200 µL of the supernatant into a new 1.5 mL tube, then add the following precipitation reagents:

| Precipitation Component | Volume |
| :--- | :---: |
| 3 M Sodium Acetate (pH 5.2, RNA grade) | 20 µL |
| Glycogen (10 mg/mL) | 1 µL |
| Absolute Ethanol (100%) | 500 µL |

* Mix thoroughly by inverting and incubate at -20°C overnight.
* **6)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C.
* **7)** Carefully remove the supernatant without disturbing the pellet, add 1 mL of 70% DEPC-treated ethanol, and centrifuge for 1 minute.
* **8)** Repeat step 7 (the ethanol wash step) once more.
* **9)** Air-dry the ethanol pellet, resuspend in 50 µL of DEPC-treated Distilled Water (D.W.), and store at -20°C.


## Chemical Profiles & Reagent Characterization

---

### DEPC (Diethyl Pyrocarbonate)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5$ | **MW:** 162.14 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Chemical treatment for RNase inactivation in aqueous reagents and plasticware.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Strong electrophilic alkylating agent; liquid density of 1.12 g/mL at 20°C.
  * Hydrolyzes in aqueous solution to form ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), accelerated by thermal autoclaving ($121\text{ °C}$ for 15–20 min).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Covalently modifies active-site histidine residues (via carbethoxylation) and other nucleophilic groups in RNase enzymes, causing irreversible denaturation and loss of catalytic activity.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Potentially carcinogenic, volatile, and a moisture-sensitive lachrymator.
  * Must be completely decomposed via autoclaving prior to reagent use; residual active DEPC will carbboxylate adenine residues on purified RNA, inhibiting downstream reverse transcription and enzymatic reactions.

---

### TRIzol Reagent (Guanidinium Thiocyanate & Phenol Solution)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** Monophasic aqueous solution containing Guanidinium Thiocyanate ($\text{CH}_6\text{N}_3\text{SCN}$, MW: 118.16 g/mol) and Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$, MW: 94.11 g/mol) at low pH ($\approx 4.0$).
* **Role in Protocol:** Simultaneous tissue lysis, cellular protein denaturation, and selective RNA partitioning.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * High-density, acidic monophasic solution ($d \approx 1.2 \text{ g/mL}$) stabilized with buffer salts and red dye.
  * Strong chaotropic potential combined with organic solvent solubility.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Guanidinium thiocyanate acts as a potent chaotrope that disrupts protein secondary and tertiary structures, immediately inactivating endogenous RNases. Acidic phenol maintains DNA in a non-ionized state, causing genomic DNA and denatured proteins to partition into the organic phase/interphase upon chloroform addition, while RNA remains protonated and soluble in the upper aqueous phase.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly toxic, corrosive, and rapidly absorbed through skin; causes severe chemical burns and systemic toxicity.
  * Handle strictly inside a certified chemical fume hood while wearing appropriate nitrile personal protective equipment (PPE).

---

### Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{CHCl}_3$ | **MW:** 119.38 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Organic phase separator and lipid-protein extractant.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Dense, volatile, halogenated organic solvent ($d = 1.49 \text{ g/cm}^3$ at 25°C).
  * Immiscible with water; forms a dense lower organic layer.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Increases the density shift between the organic and aqueous layers during extraction, forcing a sharp partition interface. Denatures remaining hydrophobic proteins and isolates lipids into the lower organic phase, allowing clean recovery of the hydrophilic upper aqueous RNA phase.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Suspected human carcinogen, hepatotoxin, and central nervous system depressant.
  * Highly volatile; dispense exclusively under a chemical fume hood. Avoid contact with non-compatible plastics (e.g., polystyrene).

---

### Isopropanol (2-Propanol / Isopropyl Alcohol)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}$ | **MW:** 60.10 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Primary nucleic acid precipitating solvent.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Polar secondary alcohol with a dielectric constant ($\epsilon \approx 18.3$) significantly lower than water ($\epsilon \approx 80$).
  * Fully miscible in aqueous solutions.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Decreases the hydration shell around RNA molecules by displacing water molecules. Lowered dielectric constant promotes electrostatic interaction between trace cations and the negatively charged phosphate backbone ($-\text{PO}_4^-$), driving rapid conformational collapse and quantitative RNA precipitation at room temperature.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly flammable liquid and vapor; keep away from heat, sparks, and open flames.
  * Causes severe eye irritation and central nervous system depression (dizziness, drowsiness) if inhaled.

---

### Ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$ | **MW:** 46.07 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Desalting pellet wash agent (70–75% v/v) and high-efficiency co-precipitant (100%).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Primary aliphatic alcohol; dielectric constant $\epsilon \approx 24.5$ at 25°C.
  * Volatile with a boiling point of 78.37°C.
* **Mechanism of Action:** At 70–75% concentration, ethanol dissolves residual co-precipitated monovalent salts ($\text{Na}^+$, guanidinium ions) while preserving the insoluble, hydrated structural state of the pelleted RNA core. At absolute concentrations (100%), it reduces dielectric permittivity to precipitate fine nucleic acids from aqueous solutions in the presence of monovalent cations.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly flammable; store in designated flammable cabinets.
  * Ensure complete air-drying of the pellet prior to resuspension to prevent residual ethanol from interfering with downstream enzymatic assays.

---

### PCI (Phenol : Chloroform : Isoamyl Alcohol 25:24:1 v/v/v)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** Composite organic solvent mixture of Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$), and Isoamyl Alcohol ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}\text{O}$, MW: 88.15 g/mol).
* **Role in Protocol:** Secondary purification reagent for DNase enzyme removal and complete protein clearance post-digestion.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Heavy organic mixture ($d > 1.2 \text{ g/mL}$) with distinct hydrophobic partitioning characteristics.
  * Isoamyl alcohol acts as a surfactant/anti-foaming agent.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Phenol rapidly denatures residual enzymatic proteins (TURBO DNase) at the liquid interface; Chloroform stabilizes the organic phase, accelerates phase separation, and extracts trace phenol from the aqueous layer. Isoamyl alcohol prevents foaming during vigorous inversion and stabilizes the interphase layer.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly corrosive and toxic; causes immediate severe chemical burns and systemic toxicity via dermal absorption.
  * Collect reagent strictly from the lower organic phase underneath the protective upper aqueous layer using appropriate chemical-resistant tips inside a fume hood.

---

### Sodium Acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$, pH 5.2)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ | **MW:** 82.03 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Monovalent salt additive for controlled nucleic acid ethanol precipitation (3 M stock, pH 5.2).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Soluble organic sodium salt forming a weak conjugate base buffer system.
  * Fully dissociates into $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$ ions in aqueous media.
* **Mechanism of Action:** $\text{Na}^+$ cations neutralize the negative electrostatic charges on the polyanionic phosphodiester backbone of RNA, eliminating inter-chain repulsion and allowing the ethanol solvent to efficiently aggregate and precipitate the nucleic acid polymers.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Mild skin, eye, and respiratory tract irritant.
  * Hygroscopic; keep container tightly closed in a dry environment.

---

### Glycogen

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5)_n$ | High-molecular-weight branched polysaccharide of D-glucose.
* **Role in Protocol:** Inert macromolecular carrier / co-precipitant for low-yield RNA recovery.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Water-soluble, highly branched polymer ($\alpha\text{-(1}\rightarrow\text{4)}$ linkage with $\alpha\text{-(1}\rightarrow\text{6)}$ branches).
  * Insoluble in high concentrations of short-chain alcohols (ethanol/isopropanol).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Co-precipitates along with nucleic acids when exposed to alcohol and monovalent salts, forming a visible, physically stable pellet matrix that encapsulates micro-quantities of RNA, preventing sample loss during supernatant decanting.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Non-hazardous and biologically inert; does not interfere with downstream reverse transcription, PCR, or spectrophotometric readings at $A_{260/280}$.
  * Store at -20°C in sterile aliquots to avoid microbial contamination or degradation.



## 3. Tomato DNA Extraction


## Reference Protocol

### 1. Genomic DNA Extraction (CTAB Method)

* **1)** Prepare sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes (e-tubes) and collect the tomato leaf samples into each designated tube. 
* **2)** Add 1 mL of CTAB Buffer and 5 µL of $\beta$-Mercaptoethanol to each tube, then thoroughly grind the leaf tissue sample.
* **3)** Incubate the mixtures at 65°C for 1 hour using a digital dry bath or water bath. 
    * *Note:* To ensure uniform lysis, invert the tubes 10 times every 10 minutes throughout the incubation period.
* **4)** Vortex the incubated samples thoroughly.
* **5)** Centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. 
    * *Note:* Evaluate the pellet and phase separation state after the run; an additional 3 minutes of centrifugation may be applied if the sample state requires further clarification.
* **6)** Carefully transfer 700 µL of the cleared supernatant to a new 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tube.
* **7)** Add 700 µL of PCI (Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol = 25:24:1) to the recovered supernatant to achieve a 1:1 volumetric ratio.
* **8)** Mix the solution completely by inverting the tube 10 times, then centrifuge at 13,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C.
* **9)** Recover 500 µL of the upper aqueous supernatant and transfer it into a fresh 2.0 mL microcentrifuge tube.
* **10)** Add an equal volume (500 µL) of PCI directly to the transferred supernatant.
* **11)** Mix by inverting the tube 10 times, then centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C.
* **12)** Transfer exactly 450 µL of the final clear aqueous supernatant into a new 1.5 mL e-tube.
* **13)** Add 450 µL of Isopropanol (Isopropyl Alcohol) to maintain a precise 1:1 ratio, and mix thoroughly by inverting.
* **14)** Incubate the final precipitation mixture at -20°C overnight to maximize DNA yield.


## Chemical Profiles & Reagent Characterization

---

### CTAB (Cetyltrimethylammonium Bromide)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_{19}\text{H}_{42}\text{BrN}$ | **MW:** 364.45 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Primary cationic detergent and plant tissue cell lysis surfactant[cite: 3].
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Cationic quaternary ammonium surfactant containing a hydrophobic 16-carbon alkyl chain and a positively charged head group.
  * Soluble in aqueous media at elevated temperatures; forms micellar structures above its critical micelle concentration (CMC $\approx 1 \text{ mM}$ at 25°C).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Solubilizes lipid membranes and plant cell wall components during thermal lysis ($65\text{ °C}$)[cite: 3]. Under high ionic strength conditions ($\text{NaCl} > 0.7 \text{ M}$), CTAB forms soluble complexes with nucleic acids while maintaining polysaccharides, polyphenols, and denatured proteins in a soluble state or selectively precipitating complex carbohydrates.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Causes severe eye damage and skin irritation; toxic to aquatic organisms.
  * Tends to precipitate out of solution at low temperatures ($<15\text{ °C}$); warm stock solution gently prior to use to fully re-dissolve.

---

### $\beta$-Mercaptoethanol (2-Mercaptoethanol / BME)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{OS}$ | **MW:** 78.13 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Antioxidant reducing agent in CTAB lysis buffer to prevent polyphenol oxidation[cite: 3].
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Organosulfur thiol compound; clear liquid with high vapor pressure and a pungent, disagreeable odor.
  * Strong reducing agent capable of cleaving disulfide bonds ($-\text{S}\text{--}\text{S}-$).
* **Mechanism of Action:** Reduces polyphenol oxidase enzymes and endogenous plant polyphenols released during tissue homogenization[cite: 3]. Prevents the auto-oxidation of polyphenols into reactive quinones, which covalently cross-link with genomic DNA and permanently contaminate purified extracts.
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly toxic via inhalation, dermal absorption, and ingestion; potent lachrymator and environmental hazard.
  * Must be dispensed strictly inside a certified chemical fume hood[cite: 3]. Add fresh to CTAB buffer immediately prior to tissue homogenization.

---

### PCI (Phenol : Chloroform : Isoamyl Alcohol 25:24:1 v/v/v)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** Composite organic solvent mixture of Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$), and Isoamyl Alcohol ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}\text{O}$)[cite: 3].
* **Role in Protocol:** Biphasic organic extraction reagent for multi-stage protein removal and cell lysate clarification[cite: 3].
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * High-density organic liquid mixture ($d > 1.2 \text{ g/mL}$) forming an immiscible lower organic layer when combined with aqueous lysates[cite: 3].
* **Mechanism of Action:** Phenol denatures residual proteins at the liquid interface[cite: 3]; Chloroform stabilizes the organic phase, promotes phase separation, and extracts trace phenol from the aqueous layer[cite: 3]; Isoamyl alcohol functions as an anti-foaming agent to stabilize the interface[cite: 3]. High-molecular-weight genomic DNA remains selectively partitioned in the upper aqueous phase[cite: 3].
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Highly corrosive and toxic; causes immediate chemical burns and systemic toxicity upon contact.
  * Collect reagent exclusively from the lower organic phase underneath the protective upper aqueous layer inside a fume hood[cite: 3].

---

### Isopropanol (2-Propanol / Isopropyl Alcohol)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}$ | **MW:** 60.10 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Genomic DNA precipitating alcohol[cite: 3].
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Polar secondary alcohol; dielectric constant $\epsilon \approx 18.3$ at 25°C.
  * Highly volatile and fully miscible in water.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Reduces the dielectric constant of the aqueous solution, disrupting the hydration shell surrounding high-molecular-weight genomic DNA[cite: 3]. Promotes electrostatic interactions between cations ($\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$) and the negatively charged phosphodiester backbone, driving structural aggregation and quantitative DNA precipitation[cite: 3].
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Flammable liquid and vapor; keep away from heat sources and open flames.
  * Vapors cause drowsiness, dizziness, and central nervous system depression if inhaled.

---

### Ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$, 70% Solution)

* **Chemical Identity & Formula:** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$ | **MW:** 46.07 g/mol
* **Role in Protocol:** Equipment sanitization and surface decontamination agent between sample grinding steps[cite: 3].
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * Primary aliphatic alcohol diluted with water (70% v/v); volatile solvent.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Solubilizes cellular lipids and denatures residual nucleic acids and proteins on mechanical drill tips between samples, preventing cross-sample tissue carryover and contamination across independent replicates[cite: 3].
* **Handling & Safety Cautions:**
  * Flammable; keep away from electrical equipment spark hazards during drill operation.
  * Store wipes or stock solutions in sealed containers to prevent evaporation and loss of concentration.
